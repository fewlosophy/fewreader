The Last Lighthouse Keeper

The storm had been building for three days before it finally broke over the island.
Mara pressed her face against the cold glass of the lantern room and watched the waves
claw at the rocks below, each one higher than the last. The light rotated behind her
with its slow, patient rhythm — forty years of the same rotation, the same beam, the
same darkness between flashes.

She had inherited the lighthouse from her father, who had inherited it from his.
Neither of them had ever wanted to leave. She understood that now in a way she hadn't
when she was young and furious at the smallness of island life. The sea demanded a
witness. Someone had to stay.

By morning the storm had passed and the water lay flat and silver, as though nothing
had happened. A container ship appeared on the horizon, impossibly large, moving with
the slow gravity of something that had forgotten it could stop. Mara watched it until
it disappeared, then went downstairs to make coffee. The light still turned. It always
turned. That was the whole point.
